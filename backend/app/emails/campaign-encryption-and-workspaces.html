01  —  End-to-end encryption
We can’t read it. Not “won’t” — can’t.
Tick one box when you publish and the document — title included — is sealed in your browser with AES-256-GCM before anything is sent.
🔑
The key lives in the link, after the # — the one part of a URL browsers never send to a server and strip from referrers. It reaches no log, no database, nothing of ours. You can watch a network tab and confirm that yourself.
🔁
Rotate the key whenever a link has travelled further than you meant it to, and the old one stops opening the document from that moment.
🗄️
A database dump yields bytes. We store ciphertext we have no key for, so a subpoena or a rogue operator gets exactly that and nothing more.
What it does not do
The link is the key. Anyone you forward it to can read the document, and so can anyone who finds it in your browser history. Encryption takes us out of the set of people who can read your document — it doesn’t take out anyone you send it to. There’s also no recovery: logging in gives you control of the record, never the contents.
